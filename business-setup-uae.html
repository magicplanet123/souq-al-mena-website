UAE BUSINESS SETUP · FREE-ZONE PARTNER GUIDE

Know who issues your licence—and who helps you apply.

A practical, source-linked guide to major UAE free zones, their publicly named partners, and independent setup providers.

See published partner names Compare service providers

Research snapshot: 7 November 2026. This is a sourced selection of nine major free-zone ecosystems, not a complete list of every UAE free zone or every private partner. Some authorities publish only programme details or use partner portals that do not expose a public roster. Where an exact name is not published by the authority, this guide says so instead of treating a provider’s marketing claim as proof.

The UAE Ministry of Economy notes that free-zone entities are subject to the rules of their respective free-zone authorities, and that structures and services vary by zone. Confirm activity, licence, office and visa requirements directly with the authority before making a decision. UAE Ministry of Economy: establishing a business in free zones.

Start with the right category

Three different kinds of business

They can appear together in a setup journey, but they are not interchangeable.

01

Free-zone authority

The zone sets its own rules and offers its licences and facilities. It is the source to check for the activity, legal form and licence you need.

02

Setup consultancy

A private business may advise on options and help prepare or submit applications. A consultancy is not itself a free zone, even if a zone lists it as a partner.

03

Accounting software

Software can help manage a company’s finances after setup. It does not issue a UAE trade licence or act as a free-zone authority.

Labels matter. “Channel partner,” “authorised city partner,” “recognised consultant,” and “top-performing partner” are the exact labels used by different sources. One label does not automatically prove another. A provider’s statement that it can help with a zone setup is not, by itself, proof of an appointment by that zone.

Before you appoint a provider

Verify the exact company—not just the brand name.

Ask the relevant free-zone authority to confirm the provider’s current legal entity, partner category, territory and permitted scope. Check the company’s trade licence and request written confirmation of any claimed appointment. Compare the authority’s direct fees with the provider’s professional fees, and confirm what is included before sharing documents or paying.
